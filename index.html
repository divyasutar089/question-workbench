What the evidence shows

Narrative review of 11 sources, most from health fields. Numbers are as the authors reported them.

RQ1. Does question quality affect acceptance?

Yes, as an association. Weak questions are a frequent reason for rejection, but the studies cannot show that fixing the question raises acceptance.

Rejected before review with an ineffective question or design [3]92%
  • [1] 151 manuscripts: insufficient problem statement among main reasons.
  • [2] Review of criticisms: insignificant question and insufficient problem statement.

RQ2. Do PICO and SPIDER improve results?

Mixed. In a crossover trial of 22 clinicians [7], the differences were not significant.

Precision, standard queries4.02%
Precision, PICO queries3.44%
Recall, standard queries12.27%
Recall, PICO queries13.62%
  • [8] SPIDER returned 55 hits against 14,250 for PICO, and 3 relevant papers against 14. It was the most specific tool but the least sensitive.
  • [9] An alternative framework matched PICO; both raised self-efficacy.

RQ3. Does teaching raise acceptance?

No evidence found. None of the reviewed sources measured the acceptance of undergraduate papers after framework training.

The gap, and the study that would fill it

The guide on this page is a synthesis of the sources and has not been tested on students. The proposed test is a randomised study that compares students who use the guide with students who do not, and follows them into later papers.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The review is narrative, not a systematic review, and the web search was not exhaustive.
  • Most sources are from health fields, where PICO was designed. Findings may not transfer to engineering, computing or the humanities.
  • Positive findings are more likely to be published, so the picture may be too favourable.
  • The score bands in the tool (below 10, 10 to 14, 15 to 20) are a suggestion added for this site, not a validated cut-off.
